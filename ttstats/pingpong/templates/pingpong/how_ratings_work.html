How ratings work

Short version: the number is your level, the ± is how sure we are.

  1. Your number and the ±

    The number is our best estimate of your level. The ± next to it is how sure we are. 1532 ±48 means we think you are about 1532, give or take 48.

  2. Ranking uses the number alone

    The table is ordered by the number. The ± never changes who is above whom.

  3. Every confirmed match is rated on its own

    When a result is waiting for you, the "if agreed" figure is exactly what will be written once everyone agrees, unless another of your matches is confirmed first. Nothing moves until then.

  4. Not playing makes us less sure

    The longer you are away, the wider the ± grows. Coming back, a player moves faster until the ± narrows again.

  5. What the markers mean

    A rating is marked when the ± is wider than {% provisional_rd %}.

    • Provisional Few rated matches yet, so each result moves this a lot.
    • Returning Not played for a while, so the next results count extra while it catches up.
    • Unrated No rated match in that format yet, so there is no number.

  6. Singles, doubles and seasons

    Singles and doubles are rated separately. A new season starts from your all-time rating in that format, pulled part of the way back toward {% rating_mean %}. A season's ± starts at least {% season_rd_floor %} wide, so early results in a new season count for more.

    In doubles each player is rated against a composite of the two opponents, and keeps their own ±, so partners can move by different amounts after the same match.

  7. Why your number changed

    This is a new rating system, not a reset. Everyone's history was re-rated the same way, so numbers moved and rankings largely held.
